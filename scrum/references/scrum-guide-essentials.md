# Scrum Guide Essentials — the nuances people get wrong

Not a summary of the 2020 Scrum Guide — read the Guide itself, it is eleven pages. This is the set of points teams, Scrum Masters and stakeholders most often get wrong in practice, with what the Guide actually says.

Short quotations below are from the 2020 Scrum Guide by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, offered under Attribution ShareAlike 4.0 International.

---

## 1. Commitments are attached to artifacts — and there are exactly three

Each artifact has one commitment. The pairing is the part people miss:

| Artifact | Commitment |
|---|---|
| Product Backlog | Product Goal |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal |
| Increment | Definition of Done |

**Why it matters:** commitments exist to provide focus and measure progress. A Sprint Backlog without a Sprint Goal is a task list. A Product Backlog without a Product Goal is a queue. Teams that skip the commitments keep the mechanics and lose the direction.

**Common error:** treating Definition of Done as a checklist owned by QA. It is the commitment attached to the Increment — the shared definition of what "usable" means. If the organisation has a standard, it is the minimum; teams may make it stricter, never looser.

---

## 2. The Sprint Goal is the only thing that doesn't change mid-Sprint

Scope can be renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned. What cannot change is the Sprint Goal: *"the Sprint Goal must be finalized prior to the end of Sprint Planning"* and the Sprint Backlog is flexible in service of it.

**Practical consequence:** "the sprint scope is fixed" is wrong, and so is "anything can change". The correct framing for a stakeholder pushing new work mid-Sprint is: *does this serve the Sprint Goal?* If yes, the team can swap items. If no, it waits or the Sprint is cancelled.

---

## 3. Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint

Not the Scrum Master, not a steering group, not the sponsor. A Sprint is cancelled when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete — which is rare, because a Sprint is short and a goal that survives planning usually survives two weeks.

**Watch for:** organisations that "cancel" Sprints by emptying them of work mid-flight. That is a cancellation without the honesty of calling it one, and it hides the signal that the goal was wrong.

---

## 4. Scrum Master is an accountability, not a job title

The 2020 Guide dropped "roles" for "accountabilities", and dropped "development team" entirely — there is one Scrum Team, no sub-teams and no hierarchies. The Scrum Master is *"accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness"*.

**Practical consequences:**
- There is no "team within a team". The PO and SM are members of the Scrum Team, not adjacent to it
- Effectiveness is the measure — not ceremony attendance, not velocity, not whether the board is tidy
- The Scrum Master serves the team, the Product Owner *and* the organisation. Skipping the third is the most common under-delivery of the accountability

---

## 5. The Daily Scrum belongs to the Developers

It is *for* the Developers. The Scrum Master ensures it happens; they need not run it, and if they do run it permanently something has gone wrong. The PO and SM participate only if they are working on Sprint Backlog items.

**Also often missed:** the three questions ("what did I do / will I do / blockers") are no longer prescribed. Any structure that produces an inspection of progress towards the Sprint Goal and an adapted plan for the day is valid.

---

## 6. "Developers" means everyone doing the work

The Guide uses "Developers" for anyone creating any aspect of a usable Increment — testers, designers, analysts, writers, ops. Not a job title, not a synonym for programmer.

**Why it matters:** teams that read "Developers" narrowly recreate the hand-offs Scrum exists to remove, and quietly exclude the people whose work the Increment depends on.

---

## 7. There is no such thing as a "Definition of Ready" in Scrum

DoR is a useful practice, not part of the framework. It appears nowhere in the Guide. Use it if it helps refinement; be alert to what it becomes — a stage gate that lets Developers reject work rather than collaborate on clarifying it, and a way to reintroduce a requirements phase.

Refinement is likewise not an event. The Guide describes it as an ongoing activity; the common fortnightly "refinement ceremony" is a team choice, not a rule.

---

## 8. Estimation and story points are not in the Guide

Neither are velocity, burndown charts, Planning Poker, user stories, or Jira. The Guide says Product Backlog items are *sized* by the Developers, and that's all. Everything else is practice built on top, useful until it isn't.

**Implication for coaching:** "that's not Scrum" is rarely a defensible objection to a practice, and "the Scrum Guide says" is wrong far more often than people realise. Argue from the outcome the practice serves.

---

## 9. The Sprint Review is not a demo

It is a working session where the Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the Increment, discuss progress toward the Product Goal, and adapt the Product Backlog. Attendees collaborate on what to do next. A one-way demonstration followed by applause is the commonest failure mode in Scrum.

---

## 10. An Increment must be usable — and there can be several per Sprint

Work is not part of an Increment unless it meets the Definition of Done. Multiple Increments may be created within a Sprint and delivered before the Sprint ends; the Sprint Review is not a release gate. "We release at the end of the Sprint because Scrum says so" is not something Scrum says.

---

## 11. Scrum is deliberately incomplete

*"Scrum is founded on empiricism and lean thinking"* and is described as a lightweight framework, purposely not a complete methodology. It defines what is required and leaves technique open.

**The practitioner's reading:** the Guide is a floor, not a ceiling. Teams that only do what the Guide requires will be mediocre — the engineering practices, product discovery and facilitation craft that make Scrum work are all outside it. Teams that treat it as a rulebook are arguing about the floor while the building falls down.

---

## Quick contradiction-check for common claims

| Claim you'll hear | Accurate? |
|---|---|
| "The Scrum Master runs the Daily Scrum" | No — it's for the Developers |
| "Scope is fixed for the Sprint" | No — the Sprint Goal is fixed; scope is negotiable in its service |
| "Stories must meet the Definition of Ready" | Not Scrum — a useful team practice at best |
| "Sprints must end with a release" | No — the Increment can be released any time it is Done |
| "The team commits to the Sprint Backlog" | Outdated — commitment is to the Sprint Goal (changed in 2011, restated in 2020) |
| "Velocity must be reported to management" | Not Scrum — and it breaks the metric |
| "Only the SM can remove impediments" | No — the team removes what it can; the SM causes removal of what it can't |
| "The PO must attend the Daily Scrum" | Only if working on Sprint Backlog items |
