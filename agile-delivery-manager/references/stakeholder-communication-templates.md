# Stakeholder Communication Templates

Templates are a starting point, not a script. The principle underneath all of them: **lead with the outcome, surface bad news early, and always bring options**. A stakeholder who is surprised is a stakeholder you have lost.

Match the format to the audience:

| Audience | Wants | Frequency | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor / exec | Outcomes, risk, decisions needed | Fortnightly + on exception | Half a page |
| Steering group | Confidence, spend, escalations | Monthly | 3–5 slides |
| Operational stakeholders | Progress, what's coming, impact on them | Weekly | Short email |
| Delivery teams | Context, decisions made, what changed | Weekly | Verbal + notes |
| Affected users / business areas | What changes for them, when, what to do | Milestone-driven | As needed |

---

## 1. Weekly delivery update (operational stakeholders)

Short, scannable, same shape every week so people can spot what changed.

```
Subject: [Programme] Weekly update — w/c 12 January

WHERE WE ARE
One or two sentences in plain language. What is now live or usable
that wasn't last week.

DELIVERED THIS WEEK
- <outcome, not task> — e.g. "Finance can now run the reconciliation
  report without a manual export"
- <outcome>

IN FLIGHT
- <feature> — on track for w/c 26 Jan
- <feature> — at risk (see below)

NEEDS A DECISION
- <decision> — needed by <date>, owner <name>.
  If not made by then: <consequence>

RISKS AND BLOCKERS
- <risk> — impact, what we're doing, who owns it

COMING UP
- <what lands in the next 2–3 weeks, and what it means for you>
```

**Rules:** no RAG status without a sentence explaining it. No task lists — stakeholders don't care that seven tickets closed. If "Needs a decision" is empty three weeks running, you are probably not asking for enough.

## 2. Fortnightly sponsor update

Written for someone with four minutes and three other programmes.

```
[Programme] — Sponsor update, 12 January

CONFIDENCE IN THE NEXT MILESTONE: Medium
<Milestone name>, target <date>.
Because: <the single biggest reason confidence isn't high>

VALUE DELIVERED SINCE LAST UPDATE
<One outcome, in business language, with a number if you have one.>

FORECAST
On current throughput, <scope> completes between <date> and <date>
(85% confidence). Last update: <previous range>. Changed because <reason>.

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
1. <Specific ask, with a date>
2. <Specific ask, with a date>

TOP THREE RISKS
1. <Risk> — <mitigation> — owner <name>
2. ...
3. ...

WHAT I'M WATCHING
<The thing that isn't a risk yet but might be. Naming it early buys
you credibility when it materialises.>
```

**"What I'm watching" is the highest-value section.** It is how you establish that bad news from you arrives early, which is the only durable basis for the relationship.

## 3. Steering group pack

Five slides, no more. If the conversation needs more, it needs a workshop, not a bigger pack.

1. **Outcomes and progress** — what the programme is for, and movement against it since last time. Burn-up or OKR progress, not a Gantt
2. **Delivery confidence** — milestone-by-milestone confidence with the reason for each; a forecast range, not a single date
3. **Risks, issues and decisions** — top five, with owners and requested actions. Anything you need the group to decide is on this slide, phrased as a question with options
4. **Financials** — spend against forecast, and value delivered against spend. Flag any variance over threshold with the cause
5. **Asks** — what the group must do before the next meeting

**Send it 48 hours ahead.** A pack read for the first time in the room produces reaction, not decision.

## 4. Risk escalation

Use when something needs action above your level. Keep it to one message.

```
Subject: ESCALATION — <one-line summary> — decision needed by <date>

WHAT'S HAPPENING
<Two sentences. Facts, no blame.>

IMPACT IF UNRESOLVED
<Concrete: dates, cost, scope, users affected. Quantify where you can.>

WHAT WE'VE ALREADY TRIED
<Shows this is a genuine escalation, not a hand-off.>

OPTIONS
A. <Option> — cost/impact: <...>  — recommended, because <...>
B. <Option> — cost/impact: <...>
C. Do nothing — consequence: <...>

WHAT I NEED
<Specific decision or action, from a named person, by a named date.>
```

**Always include "do nothing" as an option** with its consequence spelled out. It reframes inaction as a choice with a cost, which is usually the point.

## 5. Delivering bad news

Structure for a conversation, not an email. Send nothing before the conversation happens.

1. **Headline first** — "We're not going to make the March date." Don't build up to it; the preamble reads as evasion
2. **Why, briefly** — cause, not excuse. One or two factors, not a list of eight
3. **What it means** — impact in their terms: cost, scope, other commitments
4. **Options** — at least two, with your recommendation and reasoning
5. **What you've changed** — so this class of problem is caught earlier next time
6. **Follow up in writing** the same day, matching what you said

**Timing:** the moment you believe it, not the moment you can prove it. "I think March is at risk and here's what I'm doing" three weeks early is a professional judgement; the same news a week before the date is a failure of communication regardless of the delivery facts.

## 6. Milestone / go-live communication

For people affected by a change rather than delivering it. Written from their point of view.

```
Subject: What changes for <team/service> on <date>

WHAT'S CHANGING
<Plain language. No project names, no acronyms, no internal terms.>

WHEN
<Date and time. Any downtime or interruption, stated plainly.>

WHAT YOU NEED TO DO
- Before: <action, by date>
- On the day: <action>
- After: <action>

WHAT ISN'T CHANGING
<Reassurance. People fill silence with worst cases.>

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
<Who to contact, how, and what the fallback is.>
```

**"What isn't changing" is the section people read first.** Omit it and you generate support tickets that are really anxiety.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **RAG theatre** | Amber is safe; red invites scrutiny | Require a sentence with every status; track how long items sit amber |
| **Velocity to stakeholders** | It's the number to hand | Report throughput, forecast ranges and outcomes. Never velocity, never cross-team |
| **Single-date forecasts** | Stakeholders ask for one | Give a range with a confidence level; explain once why a single date is a guess |
| **Task reporting** | Easy to generate from the tool | Report outcomes; if you can't name the outcome, question whether the work should be in the sprint |
| **Surprise escalation** | Hoping it resolves itself | Escalate the *risk*, early, not the *issue*, late |
| **Same pack for everyone** | Efficient to produce | Three audiences, three formats. It takes twenty minutes and buys the relationship |
| **Comms only when things go well** | Avoidance | Fixed cadence, held regardless. Silence is read as trouble anyway |
