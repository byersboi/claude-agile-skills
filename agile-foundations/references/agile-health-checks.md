# Agile Health Checks — team and organisation

Assessment tools for how well agility is actually working. For **team** health formats — squad health check, barometer, Team Canvas — see `agile-coach/references/team-health-check-templates.md` and the `team-health` skill. This file adds the practice-level and **organisation-level** assessments, which are the ones most transformations never run.

## Principles

- **Assess to learn, never to compare.** The moment results are used to rank teams, the data becomes fiction
- **Self-assessment first,** then challenge it with observation and evidence
- **Outcomes over practices.** A team doing every practice perfectly and shipping nothing is not healthy
- **Spread beats average.** Where people disagree is where the useful conversation is
- **Nothing without a follow-up action.** Assessment without change trains everyone to stop being honest

---

## 1. Practice-level team assessment

Score each 1–4: *1 not doing it · 2 inconsistent · 3 doing it well · 4 doing it well and improving it*. Run in an hour; discuss, don't total.

**Product and value**
- Every backlog item traces to a user need or business outcome
- We know whether what we shipped actually worked
- The Product Owner is available and empowered to decide
- We regularly stop building things that aren't earning their keep

**Flow and delivery**
- We finish what we start; WIP is limited in practice, not just on paper
- A usable increment exists at the end of every Sprint
- Releasing is routine and low-drama
- Our cycle time is stable enough to forecast from

**Technical**
- Automated tests give us the confidence to change things quickly
- We integrate continuously and keep the build green
- Technical debt is visible, discussed and paid down deliberately
- Non-functional requirements are part of Done, not a later phase

**Team and improvement**
- We plan our own work and manage our own dependencies
- Problems get raised early, including by the most junior person
- Every retrospective produces one change that actually happens
- We change our process when it stops fitting

**How to read it:** look for the block that scores lowest. Technical scores of 1–2 with delivery scores of 3–4 means the team is going fast towards a wall, and it's the most common pattern in organisations two years into an adoption.

## 2. Organisation-level agility assessment

The assessment nobody runs, and the one that explains most stalled transformations. Score each statement *rarely / sometimes / usually*, gathered from teams rather than leadership.

**Funding and governance**
- Teams are funded persistently, rather than per project
- Funding decisions are revisited on evidence, at least quarterly
- Governance asks "is this delivering value?" rather than "is this on plan?"
- Business cases can be revised without reputational cost

**Structure**
- Teams are stable; membership doesn't churn between quarters
- Teams are cross-functional enough to deliver without hand-offs
- Teams are organised around value streams, not functions or components
- Dependencies between teams are few, visible and actively reduced

**Decision-making**
- Decisions are made at the level where the information is
- Teams can change how they work without seeking permission
- Escalation is rare, and fast when it happens
- Leaders ask questions more often than they give instructions

**Customer and value**
- Teams talk to real users regularly
- Success is measured by outcomes, not output or utilisation
- We can release to users whenever we choose
- Failed experiments are treated as learning, not as failure

**Culture**
- Bad news travels upward quickly and without punishment
- Mistakes are examined rather than hidden
- People challenge senior colleagues openly
- Improvement time is protected, not the first thing sacrificed

**How to use the results:** the lowest-scoring block is your organisational constraint, and it is almost never the teams. Fixing team-level practice while the constraint sits in funding or structure is why transformations plateau at "we do the ceremonies now".

## 3. Leadership behaviour check

Asked of teams, about leadership, anonymously. Brutal, and the highest-signal instrument in this file.

1. Leaders ask us what's getting in the way, and then remove it
2. We can say "this won't work" to a senior leader without consequence
3. Leaders make decisions at the pace we need
4. Leaders protect improvement and learning time
5. When priorities change, we're told why
6. Leaders measure us on outcomes, not hours or utilisation
7. Leaders model the behaviour they ask of us

Share results as themes with consent, never as verbatim quotes that could identify someone. Follow with a leadership conversation facilitated by someone the teams trust.

## Running a health check well

1. **Contract first.** Who sees the results, at what granularity, and what happens next. Put it in writing before anyone answers anything
2. **Self-assess individually** before any group discussion
3. **Discuss the divergence**, not the average
4. **Triangulate** with hard data — cycle time, escaped defects, release frequency, actual attendance at improvement sessions. Self-assessment plus evidence beats either alone
5. **Pick two things.** No improvement plan should have fifteen items
6. **Re-run at the same interval** with the same instrument; trend is the point
7. **Close the loop publicly** — tell people what changed as a result

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Consultant-run maturity audit with a score out of 100 | Teams optimise for the audit; nothing improves |
| Results reported upward per team | League tables; honesty ends |
| Assessing teams but never the organisation | Teams get blamed for constraints they don't control |
| Practices scored, outcomes ignored | "Doing agile" with no change in delivery |
| One-off baseline never repeated | No trend, no accountability for improvement |
| Assessment as the improvement | Everyone feels measured; nobody feels helped |
