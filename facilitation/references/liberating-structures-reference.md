# Liberating Structures — quick reference

Twenty-plus microstructures that distribute participation, with what each is for and how long it takes. For session design and the facilitator's stance, see the `facilitation` skill.

## Why they work

Conventional meeting formats — presentation, open discussion, status round — either exclude most people or exhaust them. Liberating Structures are small, repeatable formats that make full participation the default rather than something the facilitator has to extract.

**The two rules that make any of them work:** individual thinking before group thinking, and a strict timebox announced up front.

## The everyday five

If you learn only five, learn these.

### 1-2-4-All (12 min)
*Engage everyone simultaneously in generating ideas.*
1 min silent individual reflection → 2 min in pairs → 4 min in fours → 5 min whole group shares what stood out.
**Use for:** retrospectives, problem framing, any question where you want everyone's view. The most useful structure in the set.

### Impromptu Networking (20 min)
*Rapidly share challenges and build connections.*
Three rounds of 5 minutes, new partner each time, same question: "What challenge are you bringing? What do you hope to get from it?"
**Use for:** openings of larger sessions, cross-team workshops, anywhere people don't know each other.

### Troika Consulting (30 min)
*Get practical help from peers on a real problem.*
Groups of three. Each person gets 8 minutes: 2 min describing their challenge, then turns their back or goes silent while the other two consult for 5 min, then 1 min reflecting on what was useful.
**Use for:** communities of practice, Scrum Master or delivery manager peer groups. Extraordinary value for the time.

### 15% Solutions (20 min)
*Find what people can do now without permission or resources.*
"What's your 15% — the part of this you can act on with no more authority or budget than you have right now?" Individual (5 min) → small groups for feedback (10 min) → collect.
**Use for:** turning a frustrated conversation about things outside the team's control into action. The antidote to learned helplessness.

### What, So What, Now What (25 min)
*Structured debrief that separates observation from interpretation from action.*
**What** happened (facts only) → **So what** does it mean (interpretation) → **Now what** do we do (action). Each stage 1-2-4-All.
**Use for:** retrospectives, post-incident reviews, end of any workshop. Stops teams jumping from symptom to solution.

## Generating and deciding

### TRIZ (35 min)
*Stop counterproductive activities by imagining how to cause the worst possible outcome.*
1. "How could we guarantee the worst possible result?" — make the list
2. "What are we currently doing that resembles that list?" — the uncomfortable bit
3. "What will we stop?"
**Use for:** teams that generate improvement ideas but never stop anything. Funny, then pointed.

### 25/10 Crowd Sourcing (30 min)
*Surface and rank bold ideas across a large group.*
Everyone writes one bold idea on a card. Cards circulate while people mill; on each pass everyone scores the card they're holding 1–5. After five rounds, read out the top-scoring ten.
**Use for:** groups of 20+, when you need priorities without a long debate.

### Min Specs (35 min)
*Find the minimum rules that must be followed.*
List all the "must dos" → then ruthlessly strip out anything that isn't genuinely essential to the purpose.
**Use for:** cutting bureaucratic process, defining a Definition of Done, deciding what governance actually requires.

### Ecocycle Planning (45 min)
*Map a portfolio of activities across birth → maturity → creative destruction → renewal.*
Plot everything the team does on the cycle. The interesting zones are the "rigidity trap" (mature things we keep doing past their usefulness) and the "poverty trap" (new things starved of resource).
**Use for:** deciding what to stop; portfolio and team-activity reviews.

### Design Storyboards (40 min)
*Build a sequence of steps for a plan or session.*
**Use for:** designing a workshop with the group who'll run it.

## Understanding a problem

### Nine Whys (20 min)
*Find the purpose beneath the work.*
In pairs, ask "why is that important to you?" repeatedly until you reach something fundamental.
**Use for:** product vision, team purpose, sponsors who can't articulate an outcome.

### Wicked Questions (25 min)
*Articulate the paradoxes a group must live with.*
"How is it that we must be X **and** Y at the same time?" — e.g. autonomous *and* aligned; fast *and* safe.
**Use for:** strategy conversations where the group is arguing about a trade-off that can't be resolved, only managed.

### Appreciative Interviews (40 min)
*Find what's already working by telling stories of success.*
Pairs interview each other about a time something went really well; groups extract the conditions that made it possible.
**Use for:** demoralised teams, and as a counterweight to retrospectives that only look at problems.

### Discovery & Action Dialogue (35 min)
*Find local solutions already present in the group.*
Seven questions, ending with "who else needs to be involved?"
**Use for:** a problem where someone in the room has already solved it and nobody has noticed.

### Conversation Café (45 min)
*Make sense of a complex challenge through structured rounds of talking.*
Rounds with a talking object, then open dialogue, then a closing round.
**Use for:** organisational change, big ambiguous news.

## Planning and action

### Open Space Technology (90 min+)
*Let participants set the agenda.*
Participants propose sessions, post them on a grid, and self-select. The Law of Two Feet applies — leave any session you're not contributing to or learning from.
**Use for:** communities of practice, unconferences, large groups with diverse needs.

### Celebrity Interview (30 min)
*Get real content out of a senior person without a slide deck.*
A facilitator interviews them on stage, then opens to audience questions. Far better than any executive presentation.

### Shift & Share (40 min)
*Spread ideas across a group without lengthy presentations.*
Small stations; groups rotate every 7 minutes for a short demonstration and questions.
**Use for:** sharing practice across teams; multi-team demos.

### Improv Prototyping (45 min)
*Act out solutions to difficult interactions.*
**Use for:** practising difficult conversations, service design, onboarding scenarios.

### Integrated~Autonomy (40 min)
*Work out where you need standardisation and where you need local freedom.*
**Use for:** the "how much should teams be allowed to differ?" conversation in scaled environments. Reframes it from either/or to both/and.

### Critical Uncertainties (50 min)
*Develop strategies for operating in unpredictable conditions.*
Identify two critical uncertainties, build a 2x2 of scenarios, develop robust and hedging strategies for each.
**Use for:** planning under genuine uncertainty; pairs well with Cynefin — see `agile-foundations/references/cynefin-framework.md`.

### Panarchy (45 min)
*Understand how change ripples across system levels.*
**Use for:** organisational change strategy; understanding why a team-level change keeps being undone from above.

## Choosing one

| You need to… | Use |
|---|---|
| Hear from everyone on a question | 1-2-4-All |
| Open a session with strangers | Impromptu Networking |
| Help peers solve real problems | Troika Consulting |
| Move from complaint to action | 15% Solutions |
| Debrief anything | What, So What, Now What |
| Stop doing something | TRIZ, Min Specs, Ecocycle |
| Prioritise in a big group | 25/10 Crowd Sourcing |
| Find purpose | Nine Whys |
| Handle a real paradox | Wicked Questions, Integrated~Autonomy |
| Let the group set the agenda | Open Space |

## Facilitating them well

- **Explain the structure, not the theory.** Nobody needs to know it's called TRIZ
- **Invitation matters more than mechanics.** A vague question produces a vague session regardless of the format
- **Hold the timebox.** These structures work because they're tight; a generous 1-2-4-All becomes a normal meeting
- **Don't run three in a row** without a break — the pace is intense by design
- **Remote:** all of them work with breakout rooms and a shared board. Pre-configure the rooms; give people the question in writing as well as out loud
