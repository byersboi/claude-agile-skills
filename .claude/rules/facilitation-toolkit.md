# Facilitation toolkit

## Principles

- **Purpose before format** — know exactly what you need to leave with before choosing a technique
- **Diverge before converge** — generate ideas individually before discussing; prevents anchoring and groupthink
- **Participation over performance** — your job is to create conditions, not to be impressive
- **Time is trust** — honour timeboxes; overrunning signals poor preparation and disrespects attendees
- **Decisions need owners** — every action must have a name and a date

## PODA framework (session design)

Before facilitating anything, answer all four:

- **Purpose** — Why are we meeting? What's the problem we're solving?
- **Outcomes** — What will we have at the end that we didn't have at the start? (tangible deliverables)
- **Decisions** — What decisions need to be made, and who makes them?
- **Agenda** — Only now: sequence activities to serve Purpose and produce Outcomes

If you can't articulate Purpose and Outcomes, the meeting shouldn't happen yet.

## Retrospective formats

### Start / Stop / Continue
Simple, fast, good for newer teams or teams with low psychological safety.
- 5 min: individual silent writing on Post-its
- 10 min: group by theme
- 15 min: dot vote top items
- 15 min: actions from top 3

### 4Ls (Liked / Learned / Lacked / Longed For)
Good for surfacing what's missing — teams often focus only on what went wrong.

### DAKI (Drop / Add / Keep / Improve)
Action-oriented from the start. Good for mature teams who generate good insights but struggle to convert them to change.

### Futurespective / Pre-mortem
Imagine it's 6 months from now and the project failed spectacularly. What went wrong? Surfaces risks and systemic issues that teams won't name in a standard retro.

### Sailboat / Speedboat
Visual metaphor: Wind = what's helping us go faster; Anchors = what's slowing us down; Rocks = risks ahead; Island = goal. More accessible for teams not used to abstract retrospective formats.

### Hot Air Balloon
Good for teams experiencing turbulence: Hot air = what's lifting us; Sandbags = what's weighing us down; Clouds = risks/unknowns; Sun = the goal; Wind = external forces.

**Rule for every retro:** At least one action must be completed before the next retro. A retro that generates only "discuss further" items is failing the team.

## Core facilitation techniques

### Silent writing (individual first)
Before any group discussion, give people 3–5 minutes to write their thoughts individually. Prevents the loudest voice from anchoring the group. Essential for psychologically unsafe teams.

### 1-2-4-All (Liberating Structure)
1 min individual → 2 min in pairs → 4 min in groups of four → share with all.
Ensures every voice is heard. Excellent for retrospectives, problem-solving, and ideation.

### Dot voting
Post options; each person gets N dots to allocate as they choose (all on one or spread). Reveals group priorities without discussion bias. Use after diverge, before converge.

### Fist-to-Five (consent-based decisions)
- 0 (fist) = I block this — I have a fundamental objection
- 1–2 = I have concerns but won't block
- 3 = Neutral
- 4–5 = I support this

Ask everyone to hold up simultaneously. If any 0s: hear the objection before proceeding. If 1–2s: acknowledge and address. Move forward at 3+. Faster and more nuanced than binary yes/no.

### Timeboxing
Set a timer visibly. Announce at 2 minutes remaining. Stop at the timebox — don't negotiate extensions. If an item needs more time, schedule a separate conversation.

### Parking lot
Capture valuable but off-topic contributions visibly (whiteboard/Miro section). Shows the contribution is valued without derailing the session. Review at end — schedule follow-up or discard with consent.

## Remote facilitation

**The remote facilitator's enemy:** Silence. Remote silence reads as disengagement. Build in more structured participation than you'd need in person.

**What works:**
- Miro / FigJam for visual collaboration — use timers and breakout rooms
- Polls and reactions for quick temperature checks
- Assign a co-facilitator to monitor chat and flag raised hands
- Break sessions longer than 90 minutes; attention deteriorates fast
- Send pre-read/pre-work 24h before — reduces time needed in the session itself
- Explicit turn-taking for smaller groups ("Emma, you haven't spoken yet — what's your view?")

**What doesn't work:**
- Asking "does everyone agree?" to a muted group
- Round-robins in large groups (kills energy)
- Slide-heavy sessions with no interaction for >10 minutes

## Sprint Planning facilitation

**Part 1 (What):** PO presents top items; team asks questions; Sprint Goal is collaboratively agreed. The Sprint Goal is the why — it should be testable and valuable on its own.

**Part 2 (How):** Team self-organises to decompose and estimate/size selected items. SM facilitates but doesn't drive. Exit condition: team has a plan they believe in, not one they've been handed.

**Common failure modes:**
- PO presents items with no prior refinement → Sprint Planning becomes a refinement session
- Sprint Goal set by PO or management, not co-created
- Team over-commits to keep stakeholders happy → sprint fails by day 3

## Sprint Review facilitation

Not a demo. A collaborative working session where stakeholders and the team inspect the Increment together and adapt the Product Backlog.

**Structure:**
1. Context (5 min) — where are we in the product journey?
2. Increment walkthrough (20–40 min) — what was done; what was not and why
3. Stakeholder response (15 min) — structured questions; avoid "any questions?"
4. Backlog adaptation (15 min) — what changes based on what we've learned?

**The SM's job in the review:** Create conditions for honest feedback. If stakeholders are only ever saying "looks great", the review isn't working.
